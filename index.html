Research

Pediatric Exclusivity and the Timing of Subsequent Generic Competition

Writing sample, 2026 · Forthcoming, Duke Journal of Economics

Pediatric exclusivity adds six months to the period in which generic applications waiting on a drug's patents cannot be approved. Using Orange Book patent histories from 1985 to 2023 and FDA approval records for 651 drug markets, I study when later generic applications are approved once a first generic has entered. In the week the patent expires, 76 applications are approved in the 351 markets without pediatric exclusivity and 3 in the 300 markets with it. In the week the exclusivity ends, the counts are 0 and 95. Application numbers suggest that at least 88 of those 95 had been submitted before the patent expired.

Protest and Mobilization Against Gender-Based Violence: Evidence from Pakistan's Aurat March

Independent research paper, Duke University, 2025 · Revised November 2026

Using ACLED event data for 135 districts from 2010 to 2024, I study whether hosting Pakistan's annual Aurat March changed local protest against gender-based violence. Under standard parallel trends I find no detectable effect, but GBV-related protest in march districts was already declining relative to other districts before 2018. Allowing cohort-specific trends, the GBV share of protest rises after a district's first march, with no comparable change in other protest. Because that estimate depends on the earlier decline continuing roughly linearly, I read it as suggestive evidence that the march halted a decline in local mobilization.

Intransitive Preferences: A Number-Theoretic Approach

Independent research paper, Duke University, 2025 · Revised November 2026

When people compare options attribute by attribute, their choices can cycle. I encode each option's attributes as a product of primes, so that dividing two codes by their greatest common divisor leaves exactly what each option lacks relative to the other. Weighing these leftovers linearly gives standard utility; counting attributes won, or ignoring small differences, gives majority voting and Tversky's lexicographic semiorder, both of which produce cycles. I characterize exactly when such rules stay transitive.

How the Olympic Games Shape Global Sports Attention: A YouTube Trending Analysis

With Alex Hirsh-de Ruiter and Hayden King · Duke University

We classify 2.9 million YouTube Trending records from 11 countries (2020–2024) as Olympic, other sport or non-sport, using a text classifier trained on LLM-labelled examples. Negative binomial regressions put the rise in the share of sports videos on Trending during the Summer Olympics at about 14%. A country's recent medal count shows no consistent relationship with sports trending.

Conditional Average Treatment Effect

With Allyson Cui, Sylvia Vincent and Ethan Ouellette · COMPSCI 675, Duke University

We reproduce TARNet, a representation-learning estimator of heterogeneous treatment effects, and test it on synthetic and semi-synthetic chest X-ray data where the strength and dimension of unobserved confounding are controlled. Adding an estimated propensity score, and a maximum mean discrepancy penalty that balances treated and control representations, both lower estimation error.

Extensions on Causally Inspired Out of Distribution Generalization

With Liam Connor, Hayden King and Vincent Li · Duke University, 2026
